GSO Classification & Categorization Challenges

Mapping a free-text Arabic sample name to a strict GSO 1016 category, across eight chemically distinct sections, carries its own linguistic and domain traps, different from the microbiology sauce/swab problems.

A. Multilingual & Misspelled Raw Categories

The lab enters sample_category in Arabic with drifting spelling — لوز vs اللوز (almond), فستق vs فسطق (pistachio), and misspelled ID prefixes (uu-pe, oau-da). categories.classify() resolves this with a 7-tier chain: per-sample corrections → product-name overrides → sample-ID prefix decode → water sub-detection → 130+ name keywords → raw-category keywords → section default.

B. Ambiguous Prefixes & Hand-Audited Corrections

A prefix like pe could mean peanut (فول سوداني) or pepper (فلفل) — so ambiguous prefixes are deliberately omitted from the decode table and left to the keyword tier. Where rules cannot be trusted, Muhannad's sheet-8 audit column (التصنيف الصحيح) feeds category_corrections.csv (~2,400 rows), which wins over every rule.

C. Mid-Pipeline Reclassifications (2026-07)

Nine product groups were re-homed after review: nuts (لوز/فستق/كاجو) moved from cereals → sweets/chocolate; pepper and dried onion moved from spices → fruit & vegetables; raisins, salad, chips → fruit & veg; sesame isolated to "miscellaneous foods" while tahini stays a spice; and a new non-potable water class was created for حوض/راكد/متحرك samples.

D. Section-Specific Traps

Jam short-circuit: jam is entirely jam/jelly, so fruit-flavour keywords (توت/مشمش) must not misroute it — the classifier forces the jam section to المربى والجلي before any keyword runs. Water collapse: every potable variant (مياه/مياة/موية) folds to one class; only حوض/راكد/متحرك stays non-potable.

Data Quality & Statistical Challenges

A. The Five Validity States

Unlike a binary pass/fail, chemistry samples land in one of five validity_status states because a result is only judgeable when a regulatory limit exists for it:

StatusMeaningCount
validis_valid == True — lab-entered or derived from limits/verdict text14,677
invalidis_valid == False — a test exceeded its limit1,101
no_limitResult present but no regulatory limit → cannot be judged92
rejectedLab marked مرفوض / unfit for analysis4
unknownis_valid is None and no actionable signal2

B. "No Limit" Semantics & Non-Detects

In a result column, N.D parses to (0.0, is_nd=True) — not detected. In a limit column, N.D parses to None — the limit is unset, never 0.0 (which would falsely flag every trace as an exceedance). Jam 2024 is the extreme case: 82 of 83 rows carry analytes with no regulatory limit, so only one row is judgeable.

C. Pesticides Long-Format Explosion

Pesticides arrive one row per detected analyte: sample metadata appears only on the first row and is forward-filled onto continuation rows. The dashboard therefore counts 15,876 rows while the sunburst collapses pesticides to 15,297 unique samples — a +579-row difference. Confusing the two denominators is the single easiest way to misstate the non-compliance rate.

D. Comparison Prefixes, Dates & Duplicates

Prefixes: >10, <5, ≤0.01 are stripped before numeric parse. Year-purity guard: stray 2026 tabs inside 2025 workbooks are dropped when the sampling-date year exceeds the file year. Header leaks & dedup: pasted header rows (رمز العينة) are discarded and intra-sheet duplicates removed — pesticides keyed on (sheet, sample_id, pesticide_name, conc), others on (sheet, sample_id).

E. Year-over-Year Failure Spikes CONFIRM WITH LAB

Two sections roughly doubled their invalid rate in 2025. This may reflect (a) a methodology or limit change, (b) better detection, or (c) a real contamination spike — it should be confirmed before it is read as a trend.

Enhancement Opportunities

🔴 Priority 1 — Data Gaps That Block Features BLOCKING

P1.1 No LOD / LOQ Columns Captured

A non-detect stores value = 0.0, is_nd = True, so "below detection" is indistinguishable from a true zero. Sample validity assumes 0 passes unless another test fails. Capturing LOD/LOQ in the schema would make trace-level judgments defensible.

P1.2 Pesticide "Invalid, No Exceedance" Rows

A cluster of pesticide samples is lab-flagged is_valid = False yet has no row exceeding a limit — they surface as "unspecified" in the sunburst. The lab should clarify the failure basis.

P1.3 Heavy-Metals & Pesticides YoY Spike Unexplained

Fail rates jumped +12.0 pp and +8.5 pp in 2025. Until the lab confirms whether this is methodology, detection, or real contamination, the trend cannot be published.

P1.4 Small-n & Single-Year Sections

hormones/antibiotics is 2025-only (9 rows), jam is 2024-only (83 rows, 82 no_limit). Neither supports a year-over-year read; both need either more data or an explicit "insufficient sample" guard in aggregations.

P1.5 Dirty analysis_section Free-Text

Spelling variants (المعادن الثقيله / معادن الثقيلة) exist in-column. Harmless today (the dashboard keys off filenames) but a latent integrity risk if that column is ever trusted.

🟡 Priority 2 — Missing Analytical Dimensions ANALYTICAL

P2.1 Analyte Seasonality & Sector Patterns Medium

Pivot failed_tests_derived by month and sector to show which analytes (lead, cadmium, specific residues) spike where and when.

P2.2 Facility-Level Drilldown Table Medium

Lowest granularity today is sector/municipality. A searchable facility table (name · district · samples · NC rate) would be directly actionable by inspectors — the data already exists in the parquets.

P2.3 Pesticide Co-Occurrence Matrix Medium-High

Which residues appear together (e.g., Malathion + Dimethoate co-fail rate)? Compute a symmetric heatmap from per-sample pesticide_name arrays.

P2.4 "Missing Limits" Audit View Low

List every validity_status = 'no_limit' sample so the lab can prioritise which GSO limits to standardise next.

P2.5 Corrections Audit Trail Medium

Visual diff of which samples were hand-corrected (category_corrections.csv) vs auto-classified — transparency for the ~2,400 overrides.

🟢 Priority 3 — UX/UI Polish UX/UI

P3.1 Export to PDF / Excel

📥 "Download Report" for the filtered view — most-requested by field inspectors who need offline printouts.

P3.2 Section-Bar Deep-Linking

Only GSO and sector charts are click-to-drill today. Make the section bar toggle its section filter for symmetry.

P3.3 Per-Section Trend Sparklines

Inline 12-month SVG sparklines inside the KPI cards so each section's direction reads at a glance.

🔵 Priority 4 — Advanced / Future FUTURE

P4.1 DuckDB-WASM Backend

Serve the parquets client-side instead of inlining ~4.5 MB of JSON — big first-paint win as sections grow.

P4.2 GSO Limit Cross-Audit

Cross-check every 2025 result against canonical GSO 1016 limits and flag mismatches — the chemistry analogue of the microbiology limit audit.

P4.3 Geocoded Facility Map

Replace sector bubbles with real per-facility lat/lon for true field navigation.

Summary. The chemistry dataset is deterministic, row-for-row verified, and already shares the microbiology dashboard's design language. Its distinctive challenges are chemical, not microbial: the five-way validity model, the meaning of "no limit", the pesticide long-format explosion, and two year-over-year spikes that need the lab's read. The highest-impact next steps are (1) LOD/LOQ capture, (2) facility-level drilldown, (3) resolving the heavy-metals/pesticides spike, and (4) a "missing limits" audit view.
